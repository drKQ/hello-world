what is actually established

Distraction during needles: solid

strong

Pulling attention away from a needle reliably lowers reported pain and fear. The best-evidenced versions are attention-consuming — virtual reality trials in children show large reductions, and passive distraction does less than active. That is why this app makes you do a task rather than just play you something nice.

Sound itself, played quietly: real, and stranger than expected

promising

A 2022 Science study found sound blunted pain in mice only when it sat about 5 dB above background noise. Louder versions of the same sound did nothing. The mechanism was a specific auditory-cortex-to-thalamus pathway, not enjoyment of the music. In humans, self-chosen music raises pain tolerance in controlled trials — though results are inconsistent procedure to procedure. Hence the threshold calibration: this app tries to be barely audible on purpose.

Vibration beating pain: true, but only at the injection site

your idea needs an edit

The device that already does this — Buzzy, a vibrating cold pack — works through gate control: large touch fibres entering the same spinal segment as the needle crowd out the pain signal. That gate is segmental. A buzz in your ear cannot close the gate for a jab in your arm; the nerves never meet. So ear flutter here is a salient attention grabber, not a gate. If you want the gate effect too, hold a vibrating phone against the arm a few centimetres from the needle.

Stimulating the ear to trigger an analgesic reflex: plausible, wrong hardware

not what buds do

There is a genuine nerve target in the ear — the auricular branch of the vagus, reachable at the tragus and cymba conchae. Transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation raises pain thresholds in humans and animals, seemingly via serotonergic descending control. But those studies use ~25 Hz electrical pulses through skin electrodes, typically for 30 minutes. An AirPod is a loudspeaker; it delivers acoustic pressure, not current, and there is no evidence acoustic flutter recruits that pathway. Treat the vagus story as unclaimed, not proven.

Binaural beats: weak evidence, cheap to include

low quality

Reviews find a medium pooled effect on pain but rate the underlying trials as high risk of bias and low to very low quality evidence. One consistent hint: exposure before and during beats exposure during alone. That is why the primer runs a full minute ahead of the needle.

Can AirPods vibrate or tickle you?

hardware limit

Not in the haptic sense — there is no vibration motor in any shipping AirPod, and no API to drive one. Apple has been reported to be exploring head haptics for Vision Pro typing, which is a hint about the future, not a feature today. What you can get is a 40–110 Hz tone in a sealed canal, which most people perceive as a flutter or tickle rather than a pitch. That is a real percept, produced by the driver you already own. This app leans on it.

The honest summary

Your instinct is right in the part that matters: ear-delivered stimulation can reduce injection pain, and AirPods are a legitimate delivery device for it. It works through attention and central pain modulation, not through blocking the nerve. Expect a modest effect — a point or two on a ten-point scale, more for the fear than the sting — layered on top of, not instead of, the boring things that work: numbing cream, a good injector, distraction, breathing out on insertion.

Not a medical device. This is a distraction aid built from published research, not a treatment, and it makes no diagnostic or therapeutic claim. Skip it if you need to hear instructions during a procedure, if you have hyperacusis or noise-triggered tinnitus, or if your clinician asks you to take the buds out. Never raise the volume to chase an effect — the evidence points the other way.
